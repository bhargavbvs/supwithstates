# Methodology

## Where this data comes from

Every candidate who stands for the Andhra Pradesh assembly election has to file a sworn affidavit
with the Election Commission of India (ECI). In that affidavit they declare, under oath, their
education, their assets and liabilities, and any pending or decided criminal case against them.
These affidavits are public documents.

We take the numbers on this site from two places built on top of those affidavits:

- **[MyNeta.info](https://www.myneta.info/AndhraPradesh2024/)**, a project run by the Association
  for Democratic Reforms (ADR), which digitises every candidate's affidavit into a searchable
  format.
- **The ADR's own published analysis** of the 2024 Andhra Pradesh assembly election, used for
  statewide totals and to cross-check individual records.

## When ADR and the ECI disagree

ADR says this about its own data, and we carry the same rule here: **where the ADR's published
figures and the original ECI affidavit disagree, the ECI affidavit is authoritative.** ADR
digitises what candidates filed; the affidavit itself is the primary legal record. If you spot a
mismatch, tell us which source you checked — see "How to report a correction" below.

## What "declared criminal case" means

When this site says a representative has a **declared criminal case**, it means exactly one
thing: that person listed a pending or decided criminal case against themselves in their own
sworn affidavit to the Election Commission. Nothing more.

It is worth being precise about what this does **not** mean:

- It does **not** mean the person has been convicted of anything.
- It does **not** mean a court has found them guilty.
- It is **not** proof of guilt, and it is not this site's judgment about the person.
- It does **not** mean the person "committed a crime" — a pending case is an accusation working
  its way through the courts, not a settled fact.

Cases in Indian politics are frequently filed for reasons that have nothing to do with the
underlying conduct — including cases filed by political rivals — and many pending cases take
years, sometimes decades, to resolve. A high number of declared cases tells you what a candidate
disclosed. It does not tell you what they did.

This is why this site avoids loaded shorthand and instead always uses the specific phrase
"declared criminal case(s)," because that is what the number actually represents.

## Why pending and convicted cases are always shown separately

A **pending** case is an accusation currently before a court, with no final verdict. A
**conviction** is a case a court has already decided, finding the person guilty. These are not
the same thing, and this site never adds them together into one number. Every representative's
profile shows pending declared cases and convictions as two separate figures, even when the
conviction count is zero — because showing "zero convictions" explicitly is part of being clear
about the difference.

## What counts as a "serious" declared criminal case

Alongside the total count, we show a separate count of **serious declared criminal cases**, using
ADR's own published definition. A declared case counts as "serious" if it falls into any of these
categories:

- The offence carries a maximum punishment of five years' imprisonment or more.
- The offence is non-bailable.
- It is an electoral offence, such as bribery or undue influence, under the Representation of the
  People Act.
- It relates to loss to the public exchequer, or to corruption, under the Prevention of
  Corruption Act.
- It is an offence against women.
- It is an offence against the State, including offences that promote enmity between groups.
- It involves trafficking in narcotic drugs.

This categorisation is ADR's, not ours. We report the "serious" count exactly as ADR classifies
it, and it is always shown as its own figure, never merged into the plain total.

## Declared assets

Asset and liability figures — movable assets, immovable assets, total assets, and liabilities —
are reported exactly as the candidate wrote them in their affidavit. We do not estimate, adjust,
or reinterpret them. Amounts are shown in Indian Rupees, usually in lakhs (₹1 lakh = ₹1,00,000)
or crores (₹1 crore = ₹1,00,00,000) for readability.

## Photos

We only use photographs that carry a licence permitting reuse, sourced from places like Wikimedia
Commons. Every photo on this site shows its licence and credit alongside it. Where no freely
licensed photo exists for a representative, we show a plain initials avatar instead of a photo —
we do not pull images from party websites, news coverage, or social media, because those are
copyrighted and not ours to use.

## The standing disclaimer

This line appears on every representative's profile and on every page that shows declared case
counts, and it is worth repeating here in full:

**Self-declared in a sworn affidavit to the Election Commission of India. Pending cases are
allegations, not convictions.**

## How to report a correction

We want this site to be accurate, and the easiest way to make sure of that is to hear from
people who spot a problem. Email
**[corrections@ssupwithandhra.com](mailto:corrections@ssupwithandhra.com)** if you find:

- A factual error — a number, a name, or a party that does not match the affidavit.
- Outdated information — for example, a representative who has since changed party.
- A broken or incorrect source link.

When you write in, please tell us the constituency name and, if you can, a link to the ECI
affidavit or another official source that shows the correct figure. We check every correction
against the original affidavit before changing anything, and every change is recorded publicly in
this project's version history.

## When this data was collected

The figures on this site were retrieved from MyNeta and the ADR's published analysis as of
**2026-08-15**. Affidavits are a snapshot at the time of the 2024 election; where we know of a
later change — such as a representative switching parties — we note it separately and link to a
dated source for that update.
