# Methodology

## Where the numbers come from

Every figure on this site comes from **a sworn affidavit** that each candidate
filed with the Election Commission of India (ECI) — for the 2023 Telangana
assembly election, and for the 2024 Lok Sabha election in the case of members of
parliament.

Candidates are legally required to disclose their education, declared criminal
cases, assets, and liabilities. These affidavits are public record.

We read them through
**[MyNeta](https://www.myneta.info/Telangana2023/)**, the Association for
Democratic Reforms (ADR) project that digitises and publishes every candidate's
affidavit. Where ADR's published data and the ECI's original affidavit disagree,
**the ECI version is authoritative**. ADR itself says so.

---

## What "declared criminal cases" means

When we say a member has **declared criminal cases**, we mean exactly this:

> The candidate disclosed pending criminal cases against them in their
> sworn affidavit to the Election Commission.

That is a fact about what they declared. It is not a judgment about guilt.

**Pending cases are allegations, not convictions.** A pending case does not mean
someone did something wrong — it means a case exists in court.

Three separate numbers appear on every profile:

- **Total declared criminal cases** — all pending cases the candidate listed.
- **Serious declared criminal cases** — ADR's own category: broadly, cases whose
  maximum punishment is five years or more. **For Telangana and for the Lok
  Sabha, ADR publishes the list of members who have declared such a case but not
  how many each has declared.** So a profile here says *"serious case declared"*
  rather than a number. Andhra Pradesh, where ADR does publish the count, shows
  the count. Neither page prints a figure nobody stated.
- **Convictions** — cases where a court has found the person guilty, counted
  from the "Cases where Convicted" table on the candidate's own affidavit page.
  Always shown separately, never mixed into the total.

---

## What "declared assets" means

Movable, immovable, total and liabilities are **exactly as the candidate wrote
them**. Where MyNeta prints a total and an immovable table but no movable one,
the movable figure is the difference between the two — arithmetic on their
published numbers, not an estimate. Where an affidavit declares "Nil", it is
recorded as zero, because that is what was declared.

Amounts are in Indian Rupees (₹), shown in crores (₹1 crore = ₹10,000,000) or
lakhs (₹1 lakh = ₹100,000).

---

## What is not here

**Vote counts.** Andhra's profiles show the winning and runner-up votes and the
margin. No source this project can reach publishes those figures for the 2023
Telangana election or the 2024 Lok Sabha, so those profiles carry no result
section at all rather than an invented one.

**A photograph for everyone.** Where a freely licensed photograph exists, or
where the candidate's own affidavit photograph is published by ADR, it is shown
with its credit and licence. One Telangana member has neither, and shows
initials instead.

---

## Which constituency is which

Constituency numbers, names and districts come from the Election Commission's
own boundaries. Telangana's 119 assembly seats are numbered as Telangana numbers
them; the districts are the current thirty-three, and each constituency is placed
in the district it actually falls in rather than the one an older file names.
Bhadrachalam is placed by hand: the 2014 reorganisation left it a near-enclave
and its centre point falls outside the state.

Each constituency also carries the parliamentary seat it sits inside, which is
what links a member of the assembly to a member of parliament.

## How we handle corrections

Every profile has a **Report a correction** link. Tell us which constituency and
what looks wrong, and point us at the affidavit if you can. Every change is in
the public [git history](https://github.com/bhargavbvs/supwithstates).
